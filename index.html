What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple measure that uses your height and weight to estimate whether your weight falls in a healthy range. It was developed in the 1830s and is still used today by health organizations worldwide as a quick screening tool — not a diagnosis.

People of Asian descent may face higher health risks at lower BMI values, so some health guidelines use adjusted thresholds for this group. BMI also doesn't account for muscle mass, so very athletic people may see a higher number than their body fat would suggest.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered a healthy weight range, according to WHO guidelines.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Because BMI can't tell muscle from fat, muscular or highly athletic people are sometimes classified as "overweight" even at healthy body fat levels.

Does BMI differ by ethnicity?

Yes. Some health bodies recommend lower BMI thresholds for people of Asian descent, since health risks can appear at lower values for this group.
